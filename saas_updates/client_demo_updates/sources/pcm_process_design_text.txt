Introduction 1/2
❑ The Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC) is mandated to plan, finance,
procure, supervise, monitor, and evaluate education-related infrastructure and service
projects in line with national regulations and established governance frameworks. To
strengthen consistency and accountability in project delivery, UBEC has developed
this Project Cycle Manual.
❑ This manual documents the policies, control techniques, and processes governing the
end-to-end lifecycle of projects implemented under UBEC’s Direct Intervention and
Matching Grants programmes. It is designed to guide the performance of all activities
across the project delivery chain and to support management and staff responsible for
executing, monitoring, and reporting on core project functions.
❑ Specifically, the manual provides clarity on:
o The policies and control techniques to be applied uniformly across all phases
of UBEC projects.
o The processes and activities involved in the full project lifecycle, from Needs
Assessment to Project Closure, for both Direct Intervention and Matching
Grants projects.
o The execution of core processes, including mandatory steps, responsibilities,
approvals, input/output documents, and key performance indicators
(KPIs).

Introduction 2/2
❑ The purpose of this Project Cycle Manual is to establish a standardized framework for the planning and execution of UBEC-funded
projects. It ensures that all interventions under the Direct Intervention and Matching Grants programmes are delivered with consistency,
transparency, and accountability.
❑ Specifically, the manual is intended to:
o Provide a clear operational reference for UBEC management and staff involved in project delivery.
o Strengthen compliance with national regulations, procurement laws, and UBEC guidelines.
o Enhance efficiency by eliminating duplication, ambiguity, and gaps in processes.
o Promote timely, value-driven delivery of education infrastructure and services.
o Ensure audit-readiness and effective documentation across the entire project lifecycle.
❑ Each process in the manual is organised under clearly defined components:
o Policies & Control techniques: Rules or principles that guide how a process must be carried out
o Description: A brief explanation of the process
o Responsibility: The departments/roles in charge of performing or overseeing the process
o Input document: Documents or information needed before the process can start
o Output document: Documents produced after completing the process
o Timeline: How long the process should take or the deadline for completion
o KPIs: Measures used to track how well the process is being performed
Note: This manual will be reviewed periodically to accommodate changes in legal and regulatory requirements, the introduction of new tools and systems, operational lessons learned, updates in
UBEC policy direction, and improvements in project delivery best practices. Reviewing, updating and ensuring the availability of this manual is the responsibility of the Director of Monitoring and
Evalaution after due approval from the Executive Secretary

Direct Intervention
Introduction
This process covers all activities involved in executing and delivering Direct Interventions Projects. This process comprises the following sub-processes:
• Needs Assessment
• Budget Preparation
• Procurement Planning
• Procurement Process
• Project Kick-off meeting
• Site Mobilisation
• Monitoring & Evaluation
• Project Closure
Policies and Control Techniques
No Policy Description/ Control Techniques
1 Needs Assessment
• All Needs Assessment Reports shall be officially submitted to the Department of Physical Planning for review by 31st of September in each
year for the succeeding year.
• Needs assessment reports shall include, at a minimum, BoQs, strategic justification for the project, design drawings, underlying
assumptions, cost estimates, geographic and site information, and any other relevant data necessary to support informed project
planning and decision-making.
• Every identified need for school infrastructure projects shall be designed to include essential facilities such as toilets, solar power systems,
boreholes, water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) facilities and other critical components necessary for a functional and safe learning
environment.

Policies and Control Techniques
No Policy Description/ Control Techniques
1 Needs Assessment Cont’d
• All records (data, stakeholder inputs, validation reports, cost estimates, prioritisation outcomes) shall be uploaded to UBEC’s central
repository within stipulated timelines.
• Project prioritisation shall be based on an approved weighted scoring framework comprising two core dimensions, Impact (60%) and Ease of
Implementation (40%)
• The Prioritised Project Portfolio shall cover, amongst others, the list of prioritised projects and intervention areas, the business case for
each intervention, the location of the projects and their impact potential, the potential benefits to target beneficiaries, cost estimates
with estimated completion timelines, and the recommended intervention strategies
• Only validated and prioritised projects within UBEC’s mandate shall be integrated into the budget.
• The report shall be submitted to Office of the Executive Secretary for review and approval by 31st of October in each year for inclusion in the
list of projects to be executed in the following/ subsequent year.
2 Budget Preparation
• Budgets must be anchored on approved needs assessments to ensure interventions directly address gaps in basic education
infrastructure, and learning outcomes
• Each phase of the budget preparation (collation, cost consolidation, internal review, approval, integration) shall be guided by clearly defined
timeframe, clear responsibilities and other KPIs to track compliance and performance
• A steering committee shall be commissioned to review the draft budget in line with procurement rules, technical feasibility, and funding ceilings
before approval.
• All cost inputs shall be prepared in line with UBEC’s standards (templates, assumptions, funding ceilings) and submitted to the steering
committee within 2 weeks of receiving the approved prioritised project portfolio for the year
• The project cost shall include the cost of supervision and monitoring throughout the project lifecycle.

2 Budget Preparation cont’d
• Budget approval and review by the management shall be concluded within 1 week of receiving the draft budget
• UBEC’s approved intervention budget shall be integrated into the consolidated annual National Education Sector budget in compliance with
national budget preparation requirements.
• Final submission to the MOE shall be tracked through timestamps and acknowledgement receipts to ensure submission within the Ministry’s
timeline
3 Procurement Planning
• Procurement planning shall commence only after the Commission receives the nationally approved UBEC intervention budget
• Procurement plan shall be derived from the nationally approved UBEC intervention budget, ensuring no project is planned outside appropriated
ceilings
• Procurement requirements must be defined clearly including scope, timelines, and methods to ensure interventions are responsive to
actual needs and aligned with UBEC guidelines and the Public Procurement Act
• The Director of Procurement shall review and sign the draft procurement plan before sending to the management for approval
• The ES shall approve the annual procurement plan to confirm strategic alignment, readiness for implementation, and resource capacity
(evaluation teams, legal support, contract management) within 1 week of receiving the request
• Approved procurement plans must be published as required by law, serving as the guiding document for all procurement activities
4 Procurement Process
• All procurement activities must strictly adhere to the Public Procurement Act, UBEC procurement guidelines, and national fiscal
discipline standards
• Bidding documents shall be prepared within 2 weeks of publishing the procurement plan by the procurement department in collaboration
with the physical planning departments. This will include, but not be limited to, instructions to bidders, eligibility criteria, drawings, technical
specifications, BOQs, general conditions of contracts, etc.

4 Procurement Process Cont’d
• The corporate communications department shall advertise procurement opportunities publicly within 2 weeks of receiving final bidding
documents. The advertisement shall be in at least 2 national dailies, UBEC website and the procurement portal in line with the PPA.
• Late bid submissions shall be returned unopened with timestamps to ensure transparency and fairness
• Bid opening shall occur on the same day as the submission deadline or, at the most, the day after, to safeguard transparency and public
credibility
• Bid opening reports shall be signed by the director of procurement and uploaded/published on the procurement portal within 24 hours
• The Bid Evaluation Committee will be appointed by Executive Management and include representatives from Physical Planning, Procurement,
Finance & Accounts, Monitoring & Evaluation, and Legal/Risk Management.
• Bid evaluation committee members will be required to sign/attest to conflict of interest (COI) declaration before evaluation begins to prevent
undue influence and insider advantage
• Standard UBEC evaluation templates (scoring sheets, compliance matrix, financial evaluation sheets) shall be used by the evaluation committee
in their assessments
• Bidders must be verified through site visits, financial checks, and reference validation to ensure credibility and capacity, this should conducted by
the Audit department
• Contract awards must be approved by the appropriate authorities (Tender Board, BPP, FEC where applicable)
• The Commission shall issue an award letter to the successful bidder within 1 week of the contract award
• The successful bidder/ contractor shall provide an acceptance letter within 3 days of award.

5 Project Kick-off meeting
• Project kick-off briefing pack and meeting notification (agenda, date, time, etc.) shall be sent to all relevant stakeholders within 1 week of
contract execution by the contractor. Key stakeholders to be notified shall include;
o Awarded Contractor
o UBEC State/Zonal representatives
o UBEC HQ units (Finance, M&E, Legal)
o SUBEB State representatives
o Beneficiary representatives
• Project Kick-off notifications shall be sent at least 1 week before the meeting and through approved channels (emails and official letters) with
evidence of receipt documented.
• Project kick-off meetings shall be held only when the minimum quorum, defined as the presence of at least one authorised representative
from each identified stakeholder group, is attained
• All kick-off deliverables shall be documented, approved, and signed by all key stakeholders in attendance as the official record for project
commencement
6 Site Mobilisation
• UBEC, through the Physical Planning Department in collaboration with the Procurement and Monitoring & Evaluation Departments, shall ensure
that all required mobilisation documentation is complete, accurate, and duly filed before any contractor is permitted to mobilise to site.
• UBEC shall issue a formal letter introducing the contractor and Resident Supervisor to the beneficiary school to authorise lawful site access and
confirm project commencement within 1 week of the project kick-off meeting.
• Resident Supervisors at the State/Zonal Offices shall be assigned to oversee project supervision, with each supervisor responsible for a
maximum of seven (7) to ten (10) sites at a time, located within close geographical proximity. This will ensure effective oversight, quality
control, and timely reporting.

6 Site Mobilisation Cont’d
• Mobility vehicles and transportation allowances shall be provided to Resident Supervisors and other relevant UBEC State/Zonal officers to
facilitate effective site supervision, monitoring, evaluation, and timely coverage of key project milestones
• Contractors shall mobilise to site within the agreed mobilisation timeframe, which shall not exceed three (3) weeks from the date of contract
award
• The Resident Supervisor shall undertake continuous monitoring during the mobilisation period to confirm that the Contractor has commenced
work in accordance with the approved schedule and applicable standards.
• Where the Contractor fails to mobilise to site within the agreed timeframe, such default shall be referred to the Legal Department, in line with the
conditions of the project contract as approved by the Executive Secretary, for appropriate action.
7 Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E)
• Project supervision shall be conducted by the relevant State/ Zonal Office’s Resident Supervisors on a weekly basis or as required, and
reports from such supervision activities shall be uploaded to the designated monitoring portal in accordance with prescribed reporting templates
• Project monitoring and evaluation shall be conducted on a quarterly basis, or as otherwise required by the Monitoring & Evaluation
Department. This will be done in collaboration with the Physical Planning Department and relevant State SUBEB technical representatives, to
ensure adequate project oversight, data integrity, and informed decision-making.
• The monitoring checklist to assess per visit shall include but not limited to progress vs work program, quality of workmanship, material
standards, health & safety compliance, valuation of works done, environmental safeguards, etc.
• Reports from monitoring and evaluation exercises shall be uploaded to the designated monitoring portal within one (1) week of the site visit
and shall include supporting evidence, such as geo-tagged photographs

7 Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) Cont’d
• An online monitoring dashboard shall be maintained for all UBEC direct intervention projects to provide real-time visibility into project progress,
compliance, risks, and utilisation of resources.
• Funding and mobility support shall be provided to the Monitoring & Evaluation Department and collaborating units to facilitate timely, effective,
and uninterrupted monitoring and evaluation activities.
• Independent monitoring and evaluation of projects shall also be conducted as required by COREN-certified firms, engaged to provide objective
oversight, verify compliance with technical standards, and ensure the quality, safety, and timely execution of infrastructure projects
• A final evaluation shall be conducted at project completion by the Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) Department, in collaboration with the
Resident Supervisor, to confirm that all deliverables meet approved quality standards, technical specifications, and contractual requirements
prior to project handover.
• The M&E department shall document and institutionalise lessons learned from each project, with structured internal circulation to guide
continuous improvement in future interventions.
8 Project Closure
• UBEC shall issue a Certificate of Completion (CoC) to the contractor only after the project has been fully verified as completed in line with
contract specifications, standards, timelines, and quality requirements.
• No project shall proceed to handover, final payment, or closure without an approved Certificate of Completion (CoC) duly signed off by relevant
department heads and beneficiary institution.
• A formal project handover ceremony shall be conducted to formally handover completed project to the beneficiary school/community within 1
week of CoC issuance.

6 Monitoring and Evaluation Cont’d
• Funding and mobility support shall be provided to the Monitoring & Evaluation Department and collaborating units to facilitate timely, effective,

• Independent monitoring and evaluation of projects shall also be conducted as required by COREN-certified firms, engaged to provide objective

• A final evaluation shall be conducted at project completion by the Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) Department, in collaboration with the

prior to project handover.
• The M&E department shall document and institutionalise lessons learned from each project, with structured internal circulation to guide
continuous improvement in future interventions.
7 Project Closure
• SUBEB shall issue a Certificate of Completion (CoC) to the contractor only after the project has been fully verified as completed in line with
contract specifications, standards, timelines, and quality requirements.
• No project shall proceed to handover, final payment, or closure without an approved Certificate of Completion (CoC) duly signed off by relevant
department heads and beneficiary institution.
• A formal project handover ceremony shall be conducted to formally handover completed project to the beneficiary school/community within 1
week of CoC issuance.
